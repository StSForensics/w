Product Liability Directive 2024/2853 and AI records

The revised Product Liability Directive, (EU) 2024/2853, applies to products placed on the market or put into service after 8 December 2026 as corrected, so from 9 December 2026. A court can order a defendant to disclose relevant evidence, and if the defendant fails to disclose it, the product is presumed defective (Article 10(2)(a)).

The date, and a correction

The text published in the Official Journal on 18 November 2024 said "after 9 December 2026". A corrigendum published on 7 May 2026 changed Article 2(1) to "after 8 December 2026". Products first sold or put into service on 9 December 2026 or later are therefore covered.

Disclosure of evidence (Article 9)

If a claimant presents facts and evidence that make the claim plausible, the defendant must disclose relevant evidence it holds, subject to the Article's conditions. Disclosure is limited to what is necessary and proportionate. Courts weigh the legitimate interests of all parties, including confidential information and trade secrets, and can order measures to protect trade secrets.

Burden of proof (Article 10)

  • The claimant must prove the defect, the damage and the causal link (10(1)).
  • Defectiveness is presumed in three cases: the defendant fails to disclose relevant evidence under Article 9(1); the claimant shows the product breaches a mandatory safety requirement meant to protect against the harm; or the damage resulted from an obvious malfunction in normal use (10(2)(a) to (c)).
  • A causal link is presumed where the product is defective and the damage is typical of that defect (10(3)).
  • Courts also presume defectiveness, causation or both where, even after disclosure, the claimant faces excessive difficulty because of technical or scientific complexity and shows it is likely (10(4)).

Why this matters for AI records

For an AI system, the evidence a defendant holds is often its activity log. If the other side cannot tell whether the log is complete or unaltered, disclosure may not help much. The Directive does not address that. It does not mention AI logs or say how a claimant would check disclosed records, and the text alone does not settle how courts will apply Article 9 to them. This page covers the wording of the Directive and cites no court decisions.
