How can an outsider check that an AI log is complete?

An outsider can check that an AI log is complete only against a declared boundary that the outsider can observe independently of the operator. A seal or hash chain shows that entries were not changed afterwards. It cannot show that an event which never reached the record is missing, so completeness needs a second, independent reference.

Unaltered is not the same as complete

Two different questions are easy to mix up. Has the record been altered since it was written? And is everything that should be in the record actually there? Sealing entries with hashes answers the first. The second needs something outside the record to compare against.

A made-up example

An operator exports the activity log of a delivery agent. The entries are numbered 01, 02, 03 and 05, and each entry is linked to the one before it. An outsider can see that entry 04 is missing, because the numbering has a hole. That gap is detectable from the record alone.

Now suppose the agent opened a door and the action was never written to the log at all, so there is no entry 04 and no hole. The record looks tidy and the seal holds. Only a second source, such as a door sensor kept by someone else, can show the event happened. The events in this example are invented.

What an outsider needs

  • A declared boundary: the operator states which actions, systems and time span the record is meant to cover.
  • A boundary that is independently observable: someone other than the operator can see at least some of what crosses it.
  • An independent reference to compare against, such as a counterpart's record or a physical sensor.
  • A result that says what stayed unobservable, instead of a bare pass.

The draft O4M Record Tests 2026 (requirements C1 to C23, public comment draft) sets out requirements for this kind of check. It is a draft and it issues no certificates.

Why it matters now

For products placed on the EU market on or after 9 December 2026, the revised Product Liability Directive expects courts to be able to order disclosure of relevant evidence. If a defendant fails to disclose it, the product can be presumed defective (Article 10(2)(a)).

Binding logging duties exist, for example EU AI Act Article 12 for high-risk systems, which now apply from 2 December 2027 for Annex III high-risk systems and 2 August 2028 for those covered by Annex I product law. Our review found that, outside financial services, none of the binding EU and UK instruments reviewed clearly requires records an outsider can check for alteration or omission. Logging standards are in progress (ISO/IEC 24970 and prEN 18229-1) and, as at 20 November 2026, our comparison of them is not finished.
