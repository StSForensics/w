Unaltered is not the same as complete: AI logs explained

An AI log can be unaltered but incomplete, or complete but altered. Hashing and signing guard against alteration. Only an independent reference can show that an event never reached the record.

Case one: intact, but missing events

A delivery agent's log is exported with entries 01, 02, 03 and 05, each linked to the one before. The seal holds and an outsider can see the hole where entry 04 should be. Now suppose the agent opened a door and nothing was written at all. There is no entry 04 and no hole. The log is intact and still incomplete. A door sensor kept by someone else is what shows it. The events are invented.

Case two: complete, but altered

Another log has every entry from 01 to 05, but entry 03 was edited after the fact. Because each entry carries the hash of the one before, the edit breaks the link at entry 04, and a chain check notices. A careful tamperer would recompute every later entry so the links look fine again. A chain check alone passes that log. What catches it is a checkpoint that was time-stamped by an outside party before the edit. The events are invented.

Why hashing alone cannot show omission

A hash proves that what is in the record is what was written. It says nothing about what was never written. To judge completeness you need a declared boundary (what the record is meant to cover) and a second source that sits outside the operator's control.

Two limits we publish

Our own test vectors keep these cases on purpose. A full rewrite from the point of tampering passes the chain walk. And an operator that consistently under-reports what it received still has counters that balance. Both need a reference outside the operator, such as a count from the provider.
