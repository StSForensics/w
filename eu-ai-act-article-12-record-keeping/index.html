EU AI Act Article 12 record-keeping

Article 12 of the EU AI Act requires high-risk AI systems to technically allow automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system. It applies from 2 December 2027 for high-risk systems under Annex III and from 2 August 2028 for those under Annex I product law. The text is about a system's ability to log. Our review found that, outside financial services, none of the binding EU and UK instruments reviewed clearly requires records an outsider can check for alteration or omission.

What Article 12 says

Paragraph 1 requires that high-risk AI systems "shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system."

Paragraph 2 says the logging must give a level of traceability suited to the system's purpose, and must record events relevant to three things: spotting situations that may present a risk under Article 79(1) or lead to a substantial modification; supporting post-market monitoring under Article 72; and monitoring the system's operation under Article 26(5).

Paragraph 3 lists minimum contents for remote biometric identification systems (point 1(a) of Annex III): the start and end time of each use, the reference database checked, the input data that produced a match, and who verified the results.

When it applies

Article 113(c), as amended, applies the high-risk requirements in Chapter III, Sections 1 to 3, from 2 December 2027 for systems under Article 6(2) and Annex III, and from 2 August 2028 for systems under Article 6(1) and Annex I. The dates come from Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026, which amended the AI Act. This page was checked against the consolidated text dated 27 July 2026.

What it leaves open

Paragraphs 1 to 3 do not say who may check the logs, or how. They do not say how a reader would know the logs are complete for what the system did, or that they have not been changed. That is the question O4M works on. Logging standards that support the Act are still in progress.
