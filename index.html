Can anyone outside the company check what an AI system did?

AI agents now act on people's behalf, and when something goes wrong the evidence is usually a log. We write public requirements for checking those logs from outside: is the record complete for the scope the operator declared, and has it been altered?

Try it yourself

Two free exercises, about two to five minutes each. No sign-up, no tracking, nothing uploaded.

Ask the Record

Pick the seat you sit in: a user, a company, an engineer, a regulator, an insurer or an auditor. Answer two questions about an AI agent's record. About two minutes each.

Find the Gap

Check the activity log of a warehouse delivery agent in three rounds. Find out what a seal can show and what only an outside witness can. About five minutes.

Short address: ftg.o4m.ai

Both are available in English, German, Spanish, French, Japanese and Chinese (traditional for Hong Kong and Taiwan, and simplified). English is the version of record. The other seven versions are machine translations that native speakers have not yet checked. The cases are invented, and the exercises are not products, certificates or advice.

Why now

For products placed on the EU market after 9 December 2026, the revised Product Liability Directive expects courts to be able to order disclosure of relevant evidence. If a defendant fails to disclose it, the product can be presumed defective (Article 10(2)(a)).

Our review of EU and UK rules found that, outside financial services, no binding instrument we reviewed clearly requires AI logs to be kept so that an outsider can detect alteration or omission.
